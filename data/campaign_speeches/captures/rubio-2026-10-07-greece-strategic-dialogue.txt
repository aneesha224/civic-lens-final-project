Well, thank you. Thank you for this opportunity to be here today for the sixth time a U.S. secretary of state gathers with their counterparts in Greece for this Strategic Dialogue. I want to begin by saying that I hope our presence here today and our work that we’re about to do – and continue to do together – highlights something very important, and that is how critical the historic alliance between Greece and the United States is. And this is especially true now at a time of great global uncertainty, whether it’s the war between Russia and Ukraine, whether it’s what’s happening in the Middle East, whether it’s – meaning in the Straits of Hormuz and, increasingly now, in the Red Sea because of the Houthis in Yemen and Saudi Arabia.

At a time of extraordinary global tumult and global instability, we need anchor partners rooted in a common history. And that is what Greece has meant for the United States and, I hope, what the United States has meant for Greece and what we will continue to mean for each other. This strong partnership, it’s a relationship that spans a variety of fronts – obviously defense and security, our mutual defense and security as allies and partners, but it extends to trade, to investment, to energy. There literally is no – it’s a cultural exchange. There are literally no areas of our societies or of our countries that are not part of the U.S.-Greece relationship, and we seek to build and expand on every single one of them.
