**QUESTION:** President Putin of Russia has reportedly, according to _The Wall Street Journal_, proposed a meeting with President Trump and with Xi Jinping next month to discuss ending the war in Ukraine. Is that something you can comment on at all? Is that accurate or is being discussed?

**SECRETARY RUBIO:** Well, I don’t know. I mean, I think there’s been talks of a meeting. I don’t know anything’s been finalized. But the President has been clear he’d like the war in Ukraine and Russia to end. He wants that war to end. He has said that when he was a candidate. He said it never would have happened if he’d been president at the time, but it did happen. And now we’ve done everything we can and will continue to do everything we can to help facilitate the end of that war.

No one is winning that war. People are dying every day. Things are being blown up every day. Things are being destroyed every day. And he’s talked about it. He’s talked about the death toll. If you listen to the President, he repeatedly cites the 25,000 people a month dying in these wars, the amount of destruction that’s occurred, the amount of economic damage that’s occurring.

So as you have seen, we have engaged repeatedly over the last almost two years now to try to bring about or facilitate an end to this war, and I think the President is open to anything that he can do to help bring the war to an end if it’s possible. And we’ll continue to look for opportunities to do that.

So far, obviously, we haven’t made the kind of progress we would like to have made, but it’s not for lack of trying, and the President remains very committed to it. You saw his post overnight, or overnight our time at some point on the East Coast in the United States, where he once again reiterated why it is that he has done the things he’s done with suspending the sanctions for 60 days. It’s because he wants to see if there’s an opportunity for the talks that are going on right now with both Ukraine and Russia to lead to something positive and avoid another disastrous year of war and conflict.

In the end, the evidence will be whether a deal gets done or not, whether both sides are willing to give in order to end this war. But you just have to keep trying to do it. Sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn’t, but that’s the nature of diplomacy.

President Trump has called for elections in Ukraine. Do you think that the relationship between the two administrations at this point is fundamentally broken? **SECRETARY RUBIO:** Yeah, look, on Starshield is something that the Department of War controls, and Ukraine has benefited from it. As far as the relationship, the relationship – it’s very simple. Our goal there is to end this war. That’s what we want to do. We want the war to end. We see nothing good coming from this war, and we see a lot of downside to it, including the very real risk of escalation and a spread, of this thing spreading into another country because of a failed sabotage attack, because of drones that are hit with electronic warfare and they wander into a neighboring country. We see that every night.

Part of drone defenses is you disorient the drone; and so the drone doesn’t hit its intended target, but it winds up hitting somewhere. And if it’s a neighboring country and a neighboring country gets hit, you can see where these things can escalate. It’s a very dangerous conflict – not just dangerous for Ukraine and Russia but dangerous for the world and for the countries of Europe, especially in Eastern Europe.

So we think there is great benefit to bringing it to an end, and we are doing everything we possibly can to try to bring both sides together. It has not been easy because this is a war that, as I said, is the underlying factors that led to it and that have persisted are not easy ones to address, but we’re going to continue to try. And if we can play a positive role, that really is the point of our relationship is to try to bring this war to an end. That’s what the President wants. The President has said repeatedly he wants this war to end and he’s willing to do whatever he can to try to bring it to an end, even if that means having to nudge both sides to get there.
